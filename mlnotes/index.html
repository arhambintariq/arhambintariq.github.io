Introduction to Normalising Flows

1. Introduction Data x follows a probability distribution p(x). We can do two things using the distribution: Data Sampling: generate a sample $x_i$ which follows the distribution p(x). Density Evaluation: Given any $x_i$ compute the probability density at this point. Why do we need density evaluation? Used for model evaluation and selection, as it enables us to compare how well different models explain the observed data. Models that yield higher likelihoods for the observed data are considered better fitting. (how good is our model) Can be used for anomaly detection. Points that have a significantly lower probability density (lower likelihood) can be flagged as anomalies or outliers. Data generation and augmentation: By understanding the areas of high and low density, more diverse and representative samples can be generated. Understanding data distribution: Density evaluation provides insights into the data distribution itself. Allows to visualise and understand the data structure, identify modes and clusters and detect dependencies between variables. Normalising Flows Intuition: Model a complex distribution by applying a transformation on a simple distribution. Normalising Flows are based on change of variable formulas.
